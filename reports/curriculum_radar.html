The premise didn't survive testing

This work started from a reasonable assumption: skills that rise globally trickle down to Kenya after a lag, so teaching ahead of the local market buys a head start. The data does not support that.

The headline 82.6% looks like strong confirmation until you notice that three-quarters of all skills rose in Kenya over the same window. Knowing a skill rose globally buys roughly 8 points of prediction, not 83. And applying a one-year lag makes the forecast worse than chance — the two markets move together, weakly, rather than one leading the other.

What is real, and large, is the level gap: AI Engineering skills appear in 39.3% of global technical postings and 3.7% of Kenyan ones, and that gap is not closing year on year. Kenya reads as a different market, not a delayed one — which makes the programme mix below a strategic choice rather than a timing one.

Method

How these numbers were built — and where they stop

Documents, not search counts

Full job descriptions were collected once, then skills extracted offline against a 146-entry taxonomy. Search-result counts carry no date and cannot support a trend; documents can.

A comparable denominator

80% of global postings mention a technical skill against 27% of Kenyan ones — the global sources are technology companies, Kenyan boards carry every sector. All shares are of technical postings, or corpus composition alone would manufacture a gap.

Calibrated against known-diffused skills

81% of skills show a positive global-minus-Kenya gap, which is mostly source bias. The median gap across seven fully-diffused skills — SQL, Git, Java, Excel, HTML, CSS, Linux — is subtracted from every figure.

Kenyan history came from archives

Live boards delete expired postings, so a fresh crawl returns 2026 only. Twenty-four months of Kenyan history (2024–25) were recovered by replaying Wayback Machine snapshots through the same parser.

What is deliberately not reported

No diffusion lag estimate. Two overlapping years cannot locate a lag, and the backtest above shows a one-year guess would point the wrong way. The column stays empty rather than filled with a plausible number.

Known limits

Kenyan 2026 figures come from live boards rather than archives, a different collection method. Skill extraction is regex-based and validated on a sample; novel tools it has never seen are invisible to it.
